# Mashini

Mashini is a Rust die-cast car sandbox for StarHermit 1.45.1. Run `npm start` and open http://localhost:8000 in a WebGPU browser, or open this folder with the native engine.

- Tap a car to see its parts. Drag in the parts viewer to turn the model. In play, drag a car to nudge it. Fast swipes have a soft speed limit.
- The red roller boosters keep the starter circuit moving. Its green junction has two fixed lanes and a brass lever. The lever alternates between the loop and the splash jump after a car clears it. Tap the lever to select the next route manually.
- Open **Garage**, tap a picture of a car, tap the same car in the paint you want, and press the green **Go** button to place it at the start. A fleet holds up to 100 cars. Adding another to a full fleet replaces the oldest one. The red pause button stops play.
- Open **Tracks**, pick a picture, then pick the picture facing the way you want. Tap the tabletop to put it there. The green fork with a brass lever is the switching junction. Connect either exit to your own track and tap the lever to change routes. Tapping a matching connector joins the pieces at the same height. Tap an existing piece to replace or remove it. On a booster, use **− Speed** or **+ Speed** to change the motor's target gain.
- Choose **Water bucket**, pick one of the three glass bucket depths, and tap the tabletop to place it. Bubbles rise from sinking cars, and water resistance slows each submerged wheel.
- Cars teleport back to the start after three seconds in water, after settling off the road, or after standing still anywhere for 60 seconds of simulation time. **Options** in the top right → **Reset track** opens a full-screen grid of pictured courses. Page arrows appear only when the screen cannot fit all nine. After the original circuits, Ribbon Leap, Waterfall Bend, Canyon Hop, High Dive, Comet Run and Cloud Cascade grow from 17 to 32 pieces, with ramp jumps and long drops into water. High Dive finishes with a high-speed booster runway. Comet Run has two direction splitters and three splash landings. Cloud Cascade starts high and runs entirely on gravity. Choosing a picture starts that layout with one red Sprint GT. Back keeps the current layout and cars. Turn on **Toddler** in Options to hide the Tracks and Garage buttons. The setting is remembered between sessions. The layout and fleet are saved locally. After 15 seconds of idle play the camera follows a car, looks ahead through corners and pulls back for fast runs and falls. Every 15 seconds it changes subjects and picks a new angle. A fast car can get a front three-quarter shot, a falling car a high view, and a sinking car a low side view. The controls disappear during these shots. Tap anywhere to return to the tabletop controls. Enable manual camera controls in **Options** to orbit and zoom.

## Build and verification

The compiled game is included. Building requires Rust's wasm32-unknown-unknown target, the local guest SDK at `../../sh/starhermit-engine/crates/starhermit-guest`, and StarHermit 1.45.1. Set STARHERMIT to override the runtime location. Run `npm run build` to compile, package and check the game. Run `cargo test --release --offline` for the simulation tests. The engine's render check accepts `--guest --script tests/workshop.play` to exercise the touch interface.

## Models and physics

Blender model source lives in tools/cars.py. The body shell, glazing, seats, chassis and wheel assemblies have separate pivots for inspection. Four mesh sets serve the ten quality tiers, with tier-specific road tessellation and locally generated FLUX maple textures.

StarHermit’s SkyAtmosphere and VolumetricClouds render a daylight sky with drifting cumulus clouds. Cloud rendering scales from a dome on the lowest tier to volumetric ray marching on higher tiers.

Rapier advances the simulation at 120 Hz. Each car has a compound body and four wheels on axle joints. Cars start at rest. Rolling resistance slows them on unpowered track, and track walls and the junction blade steer them through contact. Moving conveyor cleats push cars up the lift, while opposing motorized rollers propel cars through the booster. Airborne cars retain linear and angular momentum.

Bucket surfaces use StarHermit’s Alembic decoder. The three mesh resolutions in assets/bucket-*.wabc contain a baked damped wave simulation. Each entry starts its own sample clock. Its impact point, direction and speed place the wave, and the heights from all active impacts add together on one surface. A curved waterline and impact-driven spray give the surface depth. On devices with scene-color and depth buffers, the water uses StarHermit's WaterMaterial: it refracts the scene behind through the waves and glass sides, absorbs light with depth and reflects the room. The polished glass rim has wavelength-dependent diffraction highlights. Rapier handles buoyancy and drag. A short motor rev plays when a car gains speed. Engine, collision and splash sounds are short clips generated with local MOSS-SoundEffect v2 and played at impact-dependent volumes. Regenerate the caches with `python3 tools/bucket_water.py`.
